# Can-do milestones

Milestones describe progress as **things the learner can do**, like "I can tell what I did last weekend", rather than word counts or scores. They're what the learner is really after, and they give the story, chat and review sessions a shared direction.

Read this file when setting up milestones (after the placement test), when choosing what to focus on, when logging evidence, and when celebrating.

## 1. The framework

Every level has the same six **strands**, so progress reads as one continuous path. The learner can follow "Telling stories" from a 6-line dialogue all the way to writing their own scene.

| Strand | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **People** | Introductions, family, describing people, relationships, feelings |
| **Daily life** | Routines, time, plans, home, work, explaining things |
| **Getting things done** | Ordering, shopping, travel, appointments, problems, negotiating |
| **Telling stories** | Following and retelling events, the past, narration |
| **Opinions** | Likes, reasons, agreeing, arguing, nuance |
| **Real material** | Signs, messages, songs, articles, films, literature, humor |

The levels are Pre-A1, A1, A2, B1, B2 and C1, with six milestones each. C2 learners don't need milestones: at that point, they set their own goals.

## 2. The milestones

IDs are `<level>-<strand>`. The can-do statements are written in English. When showing one to the learner, translate it into their explanation language, and at A2 and above also show it in the target language.

| ID | Can-do | Counts as evidence |
|---|---|---|
| PreA1-people | I can greet someone, say my name and where I'm from. | Learner writes a greeting + name/origin in a chat or role-play |
| PreA1-daily | I can use numbers, days and times in simple phrases. | Understands or says a time, price, date or phone number correctly |
| PreA1-getting | I can ask for something politely and say thank you. | Learner asks for an item in a role-play or story choice |
| PreA1-stories | I can follow a tiny story with support and say who did what. | Answers who/what questions about a Pre-A1 episode |
| PreA1-opinions | I can say what I like and don't like. | Learner says a like/dislike about food, music, places, etc. |
| PreA1-material | I can read everyday words on signs, menus and messages. | Reads/understands real-world words (includes reading a new script) |
| A1-people | I can introduce my family: who they are, where they live, what they do. | Learner describes 2+ family members in their own sentences |
| A1-daily | I can describe my daily routine. | Learner describes several parts of a day, with times or order |
| A1-getting | I can order food and ask what things cost. | Learner completes an ordering or shopping exchange |
| A1-stories | I can understand what happens in a short episode without translation. | Answers comprehension questions on an A1 episode with glosses only |
| A1-opinions | I can say what I like and give a simple reason. | Learner gives a like/dislike + "because" |
| A1-material | I can understand a short personal message. | Understands a real-style text, note or postcard |
| A2-people | I can describe what someone is like and compare two people. | Learner describes looks/personality or compares people |
| A2-daily | I can make plans: suggest, accept or decline, and set a time. | Learner arranges something in a chat or role-play |
| A2-getting | I can handle a shop, café or transport situation, even with a small problem. | Learner resolves a small hitch (wrong order, missed bus) in role-play |
| A2-stories | I can tell what I did last weekend. | Learner tells 4+ connected sentences about the past |
| A2-opinions | I can agree or disagree and say why. | Learner takes a side with a reason |
| A2-material | I can get the gist of a short article, ad or menu, and explain a proverb. | Summarizes a short real-style text or explains a proverb's meaning |
| B1-people | I can talk about relationships and how I feel about people and events. | Learner expresses feelings/relationships with some nuance |
| B1-daily | I can explain a problem and ask for help. | Learner explains a problem clearly (doctor, landlord, work) |
| B1-getting | I can describe a trip: plans, what went wrong, what I'd recommend. | Learner narrates a trip with plans, problems and advice |
| B1-stories | I can retell a story or film plot in order, with connectors. | Learner retells an episode/plot with sequence words |
| B1-opinions | I can give an opinion with reasons and examples. | Learner builds a short argued opinion (3+ sentences) |
| B1-material | I can follow the main points of a song, article or video on a familiar topic. | Explains the main points of real material the learner brought |
| B2-people | I can talk about wishes, regrets and "what if"s. | Learner uses hypotheticals naturally about life or people |
| B2-daily | I can keep a long conversation going without switching languages. | A 20+ message chat in the target language, repairing misunderstandings |
| B2-getting | I can complain or negotiate politely and get a result. | Learner handles a complaint or negotiation role-play successfully |
| B2-stories | I can tell a story with suspense or humor, and give characters their own voices. | Learner tells a story with deliberate effect or dialogue |
| B2-opinions | I can argue a position and respond to counterarguments. | Learner defends a view across 2+ exchanges of pushback |
| B2-material | I can understand idioms, jokes and wordplay in real material. | Explains a joke, idiom or pun from real material |
| C1-people | I can switch registers smoothly, from a formal email to texting a friend. | Learner writes the same content in two clearly different registers |
| C1-daily | I can explain something complex from my work or interests to a non-expert. | Learner gives a clear, structured explanation of a complex topic |
| C1-getting | I can handle a delicate situation diplomatically. | Learner navigates a sensitive role-play (bad news, conflict) with tact |
| C1-stories | I can write a short scene or story of my own. | Learner writes an original scene (e.g. for the series) |
| C1-opinions | I can debate nuanced topics, conceding and qualifying points. | Learner concedes, qualifies and counters in a debate |
| C1-material | I can read literature or long-form journalism with little help. | Discusses a long authentic text with minimal glosses |

## 3. Earning a milestone

A milestone is earned through **repeated, real use**, not a single lucky answer. The rule:

- **3 pieces of evidence, on at least 2 different days.**
- **Evidence means the learner communicated successfully.** Small errors are fine, since can-do is about communication, not perfection. If a native speaker would have understood and responded naturally, it counts.
- For productive milestones (most People, Daily life, Getting things done, Stories and Opinions ones), the evidence has to be **the learner's own words**: a sentence you wrote with a gap they filled doesn't count. For comprehension milestones (A1-stories, the Real material strand), understanding correctly is enough.
- Evidence can come from anywhere: a story choice, a retell, a chat, a role-play, a game answer, or material the learner brought.

Log evidence as it happens, quietly, in the moment. Don't interrupt the activity to announce "that counts toward a milestone!", because the celebration comes when the milestone is earned. At most, mention progress at the end of a session: "You're close on *telling what you did last weekend*, just one more time!"

## 4. Focus milestones

Keep **1–2 focus milestones** at a time, chosen from the learner's current level:

- Prefer strands that match their goals. For a learner learning for family, *People* and *Telling stories* come first. For travel, *Getting things done*.
- Balance over time: don't let one strand race three milestones ahead while another sits untouched.
- Tell the learner the focus in one line when you set it: "This week's goal: **I can tell what I did last weekend.**"

Then **create chances** for the focus milestones, especially in the story. If the focus is A2-stories, a character asks the learner's character about their weekend. If it's A2-getting, the next episode has a mix-up at the café that needs sorting out. The learner shouldn't feel tested, only that the story gives them something to say.

Non-focus milestones can still collect evidence whenever it happens naturally.

## 5. Celebrating

When a milestone is earned, celebrate it properly. It's the moment the learner sees their progress as a real ability.

1. **In the story, if a series is active:** give it a short scene or reaction that fits. Savta raises a glass (*לְחַיִּים!*), Kommissarin Albers nods and says *„Nicht schlecht."*, or the cousin's wedding speech goes well because of the learner's character. Keep it to 1–3 lines, in the target language, at their level.
2. **A milestone card** in the chat:

   ```
   🏅 MILESTONE · A2 · Telling stories
   ```
   **I can tell what I did last weekend.**
   *אֲנִי יְכוֹלָה לְסַפֵּר מָה עָשִׂיתִי בְּסוֹף הַשָּׁבוּעַ.*
   Evidence: retold S1E2 (Sep 27) · weekend chat with Noa (Sep 28) · voice-note reply to Savta (Sep 30)

   Keep the header line in the code block. Put the can-do statement, its target-language version (A2+, using the learner's grammatical gender where the language has it) and the evidence as plain lines below it. The evidence line reminds them it came from real use.
3. **One line on what's next:** the next focus milestone, or what this unlocks.

Keep the celebration warm and brief. It's a moment, not a speech. If two milestones are earned in one session, show both cards together.

## 6. Completing a level

When all six milestones of a level are earned:

- Celebrate the level itself, with a bigger in-story moment (a party, a toast, a character's compliment in the target language) and a summary of the six cards.
- Update the profile's level to that level, since the milestones are the proof. Offer a quick placement retest if the learner wants confirmation, but don't require one.
- Set up the next level's milestones and pick new focus milestones.

If the learner is clearly working above their current level (e.g. producing B1-quality stories while still on A2 milestones), offer to credit the rest of the level at once. One conversation where they show each remaining ability is enough, even if it's all on one day.

## 7. Profile format and the script

Milestones live in the profile. Only the current level gets rows, and lower levels are recorded on one line, which keeps the profile short:

```markdown
## Milestones
- Earned by placement: Pre-A1, A1 (2026-09-25)
| ID | Can-do | Status | Evidence | Earned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A2-stories | I can tell what I did last weekend. | focus | 2026-09-27 retold S1E2; 2026-09-28 chat about Saturday | |
| A2-people | I can describe what someone is like and compare two people. | open | | |
```

Status is one of `open`, `focus` or `earned`. When you can run Python, use `scripts/milestones.py` rather than editing the table by hand:

```bash
M=<skill-dir>/scripts/milestones.py
python3 $M init   <profile> --level A1+ --today 2026-09-25   # after placement: credits lower levels, adds the next level's rows
python3 $M focus  <profile> A2-stories A2-people              # set the 1–2 focus milestones
python3 $M log    <profile> A2-stories --note "retold S1E2" --today 2026-09-27
python3 $M status <profile>                                   # progress on the current level
```

`log` prints `EARNED` when a milestone reaches the evidence rule, and `LEVEL COMPLETE` when the level is done. Those are your cues to celebrate (section 5 or 6). When a level is complete, run `init --level <that level>` to set up the next one. Always pass `--today` with the learner's local date.

**Mapping placement to milestones:** a placement of X or X+ credits every level up to and including X, and the learner works on the next level. For example, A1+ credits Pre-A1 and A1 and works on A2, and B1+ credits up to B1 and works on B2.
